# detection-eval: the K3 object detector (`kitefin/detector`)

## Purpose
- **Shows:** model evaluation on QA-Board: metrics per class and per object size in the Metrics Table and Metrics Diff,
  PR curves overlaid with the reference, detections drawn on images, an HTML report per run, `qa optimize` and the
  Analysis view, milestones as model releases.
- **Audience:** computer vision and ML engineers who ship models to devices: quantization, input size, latency.
- **Phase 2.**

## What the code computes
Python, [ONNX Runtime](https://onnxruntime.ai) (MIT) on CPU, numpy, Pillow.

- **Model:** torchvision's `ssdlite320_mobilenet_v3_large` with its COCO weights (torchvision is BSD-3-Clause; no AGPL
  or GPL model, so no Ultralytics YOLO, no YOLOv7). `qa/export_onnx.py` exports the backbone and head to ONNX once
  per environment, at each input size; the weights come from `download.pytorch.org`, checked by SHA-256, and are not
  redistributed. torch is needed only for that export, which CI caches.
- **Variants** (run configurations, so they show as tags): `fp32` and `int8`, at `size-320`, `size-416`, `size-512`.
- **int8:** ONNX Runtime static quantization (QDQ), calibrated on 64 images of the `calib` slice. The flags choose the
  calibration method, per-channel weights, and which nodes stay in fp32.
- **Post-processing in numpy**, the same for every variant: anchors for the input size, box decoding, score threshold,
  per-class NMS.
- **Evaluation:** COCO-style AP over IoU 0.50:0.95, AP50, AP by object size (small < 32², medium, large), recall by
  size, per-class AP. Latency: median per image over the slice, after 5 warmup images, 4 threads, runs one at a time.

**Flags** (`build/flags.json`; defaults are the head of `main` at T0):

| Flag | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `quant_calibration` | `minmax`, `percentile`, `entropy` | `percentile` |
| `quant_percentile` | 99.9-99.999 | `99.99` |
| `quant_per_channel` | `true`, `false` | `true` |
| `quant_keep_fp32` | `none`, `first-conv`, `head`, `first-conv+head` | `head` |
| `score_threshold` | 0.05-0.6 | `0.3` |
| `nms_iou` | 0.3-0.8 | `0.55` |
| `eval_crowd_once` | `true`: count crowd boxes once (a fix in the story) | `true` |
| `bug_qdq_resize` | `true`: quantizes a Resize node ONNX Runtime can't run in int8 (a real load error) | absent |

Tuning parameters and `qa optimize` override flags of the same name.

## Inputs and licenses
There is no well-known detection dataset under CC0, so the labels are ours.

| Slice (input folder) | Images | What | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `slices/indoor` | 25 | offices, homes: person, chair, bottle, backpack, potted plant | photos, CC0 or public domain |
| `slices/outdoor` | 25 | streets, parking lots: person, bicycle, car, truck | photos, CC0 or public domain |
| `slices/warehouse` | 25 | aisles, shelves, loading docks: person, suitcase, bottle, chair | photos, CC0 or public domain |
| `slices/small-objects` | 30 | objects from the slices above, scaled to 12-32 px and pasted at seeded positions | generated by `qa/make_small_objects.py` |
| `slices/calib` | 64 | calibration only, never scored | photos, CC0 or public domain |

- **Photos:** Wikimedia Commons files tagged CC0 or public domain, US federal works (check each file's page), or
  photos a maintainer took and releases under CC0. No identifiable person as the subject: people at a distance, from
  behind, or blurred by motion. No prominent logos or license plates. Listed in `inputs.lock.yaml` (url, SHA-256) and
  `LICENSES.md`.
- **Labels** (`labels.json` per slice, COCO format, CC0): proposed by a larger torchvision model
  (`fasterrcnn_resnet50_fpn_v2`, score ≥ 0.6) with `qa/propose_labels.py`, then reviewed and corrected once by a
  maintainer in Label Studio (Apache-2.0). The composites' labels are exact by construction.
- Classes (8): person, bicycle, car, truck, chair, bottle, backpack, potted plant. COCO ids, other detections ignored.

Detections are drawn with Pillow: ground truth as thin dashed white boxes, detections in the class color with their
score, missed ground truth in red. Images are scaled to 1280 px wide.

## Determinism and budget
- Detections, AP and the composites are deterministic for a given ONNX Runtime version, thread count and CPU family;
  latency is measured. The int8 model is re-quantized at each commit from the flags (calibration takes about 20 s,
  cached by flags).
- The whole story: ≤ 12 min on a 4-CPU runner, after the one-time export (about 2 min, cached).

## As built: differences from the first draft
Numbers below were measured on the (shared, busy) 4-CPU machine this was built on. Accuracy metrics are the same from
run to run, and on other x86-64 machines with FMA: fp32 ones were the same to 6 digits on a GitHub runner, and int8
ones are the quantized model's reference arithmetic (below). Latency isn't.

- **Photos come from Flickr**, found through Openverse, CC0 or Public Domain Mark, each license checked on the photo's
  page: Wikimedia Commons' API refused the build machine's requests. They are the 1024 px renditions, used as they
  are. Most public-domain ones are US federal works (USDA, National Park Service) and archive photos. Slices:
  `indoor` 25, `outdoor` 25, `warehouse` 16 (few public-domain warehouse photos have no identifiable person as their
  subject: it's "distribution centers, loading docks, food banks"), `small-objects` 30, `calib` 40 (not 64).
- **Labels aren't reviewed**: Faster R-CNN v2 at score 0.6 and up, as the plan allowed. Its boxes at 0.4-0.6, and
  objects under 24x24 px in the model's 320x320 frame, are crowd boxes (ignored). The docs say so.
- **Object sizes are measured in the 320x320 frame the model sees, with 64 and 128 px thresholds** (small: smaller
  than the detector's smallest anchor box, 64x64), not COCO's 32 and 96 px. SSDlite320 finds almost nothing under
  32 px at its input: with COCO's buckets, AP small is 0 for every variant and the story can't be told.
- **`small-objects` holds people and cars only**, 40 to 64 px in the 320 frame, cut along their silhouettes (Mask
  R-CNN v2, run once by `qa/propose_labels.py --donors`) and pasted on 14 empty scenes, some faded towards the
  background. With all 8 classes, most at an AP near 0, the class-mean AP small was noise. The recipe
  (`labels.json`, with the silhouettes) is committed; `donors.json` is regenerated by the maintainer tool.
- **No latency claim**: ONNX Runtime's QDQ int8 SSDlite (MobileNetV3: h-swish, squeeze-excite and depthwise
  convolutions keep many quantize/dequantize pairs) wasn't faster than fp32 on the busy machine this was built on
  (within 10% on one thread, slower on 4), and a shared machine's timings don't carry over to a quiet runner anyway.
  The story's reason to quantize is size (13.8 MB to 3.9-4.2 MB, for the K3's NPU); no message or check is about
  latency. It is still measured (4 threads, median per image, int8 with ONNX Runtime's int8 kernels) and shown.
- **int8 accuracy is the quantized model's reference arithmetic, the same on every CPU with FMA.** ONNX Runtime fuses
  the QuantizeLinear/DequantizeLinear pairs around an operator into an int8 kernel that it picks for the CPU: AVX2,
  AVX-512 VNNI, AMX. With those kernels, the first seed on a GitHub runner
  got other int8 numbers than this machine (an Intel Xeon with AVX-512 VNNI and AMX): per-channel weights won back
  +10% of the indoor slice's mAP there, +43% here, and the story's check failed. So `qa/detector.py` computes an int8
  run's detections with QDQ fusion off (`session.disable_quant_qdq`) and ONNX Runtime's extended optimizations, which keep
  the plain fp32 kernels rather than the NCHWc ones: QuantizeLinear rounds and clips in float,
  DequantizeLinear scales back, the operators between run in fp32, as the graph says. That's what a quantized model
  means, and what a device toolchain's results are checked against. Latency is still measured with the int8 kernels:
  what a CPU deployment would run. Checked on the per-channel model and the indoor slice, with QEMU's user-mode
  emulation for the other CPUs:

  | Where | ONNX Runtime's int8 kernels | Reference arithmetic |
  |---|---|---|
  | this machine (Intel Xeon: AVX-512 VNNI, AMX) | 0.1029 | 0.0888852 |
  | emulated AMD EPYC Milan (AVX2 and FMA, like many GitHub runners) | 0.0900 | 0.0888852, bit for bit |
  | the first CI seed's GitHub runner (its CPU isn't logged) | 0.0808 | |
  | emulated Intel Nehalem (SSE 4.2, no FMA) | 0.1029, bit for bit | 0.1022 |

  AVX2's u8 x s8 multiply-adds (`vpmaddubsw`) can saturate at 16 bits, the SSE, VNNI and AMX kernels can't. The
  reference arithmetic is exact on CPUs with FMA, whose fp32 kernels sum in the same order; without FMA, the sums round
  differently, and quantization amplifies it on this small slice. The calibration (fp32, no graph optimization) and
  the quantization (numpy) make the same model files on both FMA CPUs (same SHA-256, calibrated on 2 images). On this machine, the reference arithmetic is
  also the same with no graph optimization, basic or extended; with all of them, ONNX Runtime's NCHWc fp32 kernels
  sum in another order (0.0889064).
- **The regression, as measured**: the first int8 model (minmax, one scale per tensor, everything quantized) loses 40%
  of the indoor slice's mAP (0.109 to 0.065: people indoors almost disappear) and 6% of AP small on `small-objects`
  (0.368 to 0.346). Per-channel weights bring indoor back to 0.089 (+37%), percentile calibration to 0.111, fp32's
  level. Small objects stay 7% below fp32 at the same commit. `tune-int8` (`qa optimize`, 20 evaluations, the
  objective also penalizes model size) found percentile 99.999, the first convolution in fp32 (it costs nothing),
  score 0.2, NMS 0.7, before `qa optimize` moved to Optuna: re-check what it finds now on the next seed. The
  `int8-tuned` commit sets those values either way: int8's AP small on `small-objects` is then 0.389, above the fp32
  release kd-1.2's 0.368, and
  the model stays at 4.2 MB (keeping the head in fp32 doubles it for nothing). That's "tuned back": the new int8
  release finds small objects better than the old fp32 one did. `main`'s defaults (`qa/main.py`) are those settings.
- **`bug_qdq_resize` is `bug_qdq_se_gate`**: SSDlite has no Resize node. The bug feeds the squeeze-excite gates'
  uint8 output straight to their Mul, and ONNX Runtime refuses the model: "Type parameter (T) of Optype (Mul) bound
  to different types". Only the int8 runs fail: story failures now take a `configs` glob (`docs-media/seed.py`,
  `examples/STORY.md`, `examples/story.schema.json`).
- **`eval_crowd_once` is `eval_crowd_many`**: the bug is that a crowd box absorbs only its first detection (the others
  count as false alarms), where pycocotools lets it absorb any number. The draft's bug (crowd boxes also counted as
  objects to find) would have cut AP by 2 to 3 times on slices with more crowd boxes than objects, not "a little".
  The fix moves mAP by 0 to +5%; `small-objects` has no crowd box.
- **Larger input sizes are worse**: SSDlite320 was trained at 320. At 416 it finds a few more small objects and
  localizes the others worse (mAP about half); at 512 its anchors no longer match what it learned (mAP near 0). So
  `ci` stays at 320, there's no `size-416-default` commit, and the last commit runs the size sweep nightly, still
  running at T0 (`sizes`, label `nightly`, half done, 1 run running: runs execute one at a time).
- **Story**: 25 commits, branches `det/confusions` (merged), `quant/int8` (merged), `quant/entropy` (abandoned:
  entropy calibration is a little worse everywhere). `qdq-se-gates` and its revert the same day; `crowd-boxes` for
  the evaluation fix; `sizes-nightly` at T0. Milestones as planned.
- **Gate rules** (experimental): `{metric: map, min: 0.15}` (median over the `ci` runs), `{metric: ap_small,
  max_regression: 10%}`, `{metric: map, per_test_max_regression: 25%}`, `max_failed_runs: 0`. It fails on
  `qdq-se-gates` (crashed runs) and passes on `crowd-boxes` (median mAP 0.19, every slice a little better): the seeder
  runs `qa gate` for those checks. It can't see the int8 regression at `int8-minmax`: the reference has no int8 runs
  to compare with.
- **Targets**: mAP 0.15 (against the reference model's labels, SSDlite's mAP is 0.08 to 0.4 depending on the slice),
  recall small 0.5, latency 25 ms.
- **Also**: a `model_version` flag gives runs their `params.model` (`kd-1.3-int8-320`) and a model card badge; a
  confusion matrix (`confusion.plotly.json`) and the raw detections (`detections.json`) are extra outputs; calibration
  statistics are cached per input size and method, so each tuning evaluation only re-quantizes (about 2 s).
- **Budget**: about 240 runs in the story, ~3 s each with `qa`'s overhead (int8 ones 1 to 2 s more: they run the model
  twice, with the int8 kernels for the latency and in reference arithmetic for the detections), plus calibrations
  (~2 min in all) and the first export (PyTorch for CPU, ~1 min): about 20 minutes on a 4-CPU machine. The first CI
  seed took 12 minutes, before the int8 runs' second pass.

**Checked on CI**: the first seed on a GitHub runner passed the fp32 checks with this machine's numbers, and showed
the int8 difference above. `optimize_converged` checks the objective `qa optimize` minimizes, -(AP small + mAP) +
0.005 per MB, means over `outdoor` and `small-objects`: the best iteration must beat the first by 0.04 or more, and
come after the 8 random points. The objective's values at the first evaluation, at the best of the random ones and at
the best were measured before `qa optimize` moved to Optuna: re-measure them on the next seed, with the solver of
`qa/tune-int8.yaml` (TPE, seed 42, 8 startup trials), and check that the gain still clears 0.04. Not validated:
latency on a quiet machine.
