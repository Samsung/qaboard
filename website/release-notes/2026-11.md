## Web app

### Automated tuning
- Set up an automated tuning with a guided form in **Run Tests / Tuning**: the parameters to tune with their range or values, the metrics to improve and which way is better (any metric your runs return, nothing to declare first), and a budget it recommends. It checks your settings as you type; the YAML stays one click away, with your comments ([find the best parameters automatically](/docs/user-guide/automated-tuning)).
- Read what a tuning found in **Tuning Analysis**: its progress, the best parameters and how much each one matters, the convergence, one plot per parameter, every evaluation in a table, and the failed ones explained. With 2 or 3 metrics, it shows the Pareto front, the best trade-offs without choosing weights, and runs any of them.
- Know what to do next: **What this tells you** says whether more evaluations would help, which best values are at the edge of their range, and which parameters barely matter. Then **Continue the search…**, **Run the best parameters…** on other tests, copy them as `preset_params` or JSON, or start a new tuning from a previous one's settings.

### Metrics
- See every metric your runs return, declared or not, in the Summary, the Metrics Table, the Metrics Diff, the run cards and the History page. Before, metrics missing from the metrics file were hidden ([metrics](/docs/computing-quantitative-metrics#what-qa-board-shows-by-default)).
- Trust the colors: when QA-Board doesn't know whether higher or lower is better, the Summary shows changes in gray and the Metrics Diff shades cells in blue by how much they changed, never green or red. Hover a metric's name to see why, and follow the Summary's link to declare it ([user guide](/docs/user-guide/metrics#summary)). In the Metrics Table, metrics without a target are shaded in blue by how each value ranks among the runs, so outliers stand out.
- Get useful defaults without configuration: without `summary_metrics`, the Summary shows the 8 metrics that changed the most vs the reference; without `main_metrics`, run cards show their first metrics and the History page plots any metric. With hundreds of metrics, tables show the declared ones and others up to 20 columns, and say how many more you can pick.

### Image viewer
- Click a pixel to pin its values, so you can select and copy them: they stay while you move the mouse and switch modes, until **Esc** or a click on the **Pinned** tag. Shift+click pins in **Single** mode, where a click shows the other image, and in videos, where the pinned values follow the frames. A **dec**/**hex** toggle switches the base ([pixel values](/docs/user-guide/image-viewer#pixel-values), [in videos](/docs/user-guide/video-viewer#pixel-values)).
- Look for differences of a given size in **Where they differ**, also in all the runs: type the smallest and largest size in pixels, e.g. to find small defects next to a large change. Each region says the size of its difference ([find where images differ](/docs/user-guide/image-viewer#find-where-images-differ)).
- See the regions of interest outlined on the images, with their labels: click a label to zoom on its region, press **o** to hide them ([regions of interest](/docs/user-guide/image-viewer#find-where-images-differ)).
- See the annotations people made on a run's input in your annotation app, when your server is connected to one: outlined on the images, colored by workspace, with a link to open each in the app. Pick the workspaces you see, yours by default ([annotations](/docs/user-guide/image-viewer#annotations)).
- Work with raw images: **Guess from the view**, in the **Sensor** dialog, now applies its CFA guess, also over the metadata, and says what the pixels show and where they were read. Drag on the histogram of **Image values** to highlight a range of the raw values on the image, per sensor channel, e.g. **R** alone ([raw images](/docs/user-guide/image-viewer#image-values)).

### Run cards and logs
- Tell runs apart when a batch runs several batches of *qaboard.yaml* on the same inputs: when the runs shown come from several batches or databases, their names start with them, e.g. *night › kitti › scenes/0001.png*, or are only them when all the runs have the same input, e.g. *night* and *day*, in the run cards, the logs, the tables and the failed runs. Choose **Always** or **Never** instead in **Batch & database in names**, in a run's menu, the View options' **Display** section or **Settings → Preferences** ([run cards](/docs/user-guide/commit-page#run-cards)).
- Hover a run's name in the logs and the tables to see what QA-Board knows about it, like on the run cards: its **History**, batch, database, input path, metadata and run info. The run cards' **⋯** menu, like the logs', shows it too, before the actions ([logs](/docs/user-guide/logs#reading-a-log)).
- Sort runs by batch, database, status (failed first) or configuration too, in the View options' **Sort runs**. When the runs come from several batches, they're grouped by batch by default; names sort A to Z and "naturally" (`frame2` before `frame10`), with ties sorted by name ([view options](/docs/user-guide/commit-page#view-options)).
- Pick **Compact** run cards, next to the title of the **Visualizations** and **Output Files** views: one line per run, like the logs, with the configuration as text (hover it for the tags), and less room around the visualizations ([compact cards](/docs/user-guide/commit-page#run-cards)).
- See when a run was done again: its card is tagged **attempt 2**, **attempt 3**..., and hovering the tag says when the earlier attempts ran, who ran them, how they ended and what they measured. Their logs are in the Logs view, next to the run's. The card shows the latest attempt, and images from an earlier attempt no longer come back from a cache; for runs done again in the same folder, the image viewer warns that images may be stale ([run cards](/docs/user-guide/commit-page#run-cards)).

### Text diffs
- Read text diffs without scrolling through what didn't change: the unchanged lines are folded, with a few lines of context around each change, and the diff is only as tall as what it shows. Click a fold to show its lines ([text diffs](/docs/user-guide/other-viewers#text)). Reviewing changes to batch files in **Available Tests** folds them too.

### Tabs and links
- Open the sidebar's results views (Summary, Metrics Table, Visualizations, Logs…) in a new tab with Ctrl/Cmd+click or a middle-click. Right-click copies their link.
- Tell your browser tabs apart: they are named after what they show, then the project (*ISP v2 vs baseline · isp*). While runs are pending the title counts those done (*12/40*), then how many failed (*3✕*), and the tab's icon gets a dot: yellow while runs are pending, red once one failed, green when they finished while you were away.
- Copy a link that reads well in Teams, Outlook or Confluence: the **link** button in the commit's bar, above the commit list and in the milestone dialog copies a link that pastes as the page's name, the commit and the runs' status, or as the URL where only text goes ([sharing](/docs/user-guide/sharing#links-are-shareable)).

### When something goes wrong
- Keep working when part of a page crashes: a viewer that fails says what file it couldn't show, in the run's card, and the rest of the page keeps working, with the sidebar and navbar. A code editor that crashes keeps your text and your unsaved batches.
- Get back in one click: **Try again**, **Reload the page** or **Go back**. Pages that were open during an update reload by themselves to get the new version.
- Know when the maintainers were told: the error page says so when your server reports errors, and lets you add what you were doing. Otherwise **Report the problem** opens an email or an issue with the details filled in ([report a bug](/docs/user-guide/sharing#bug-reports-and-support)).

### Results in scripts
- Work on the runs you see from a script: **Script**, next to **Export** in the **Visualizations** and **Output Files** views, shows a Python snippet or a shell command for the runs shown, from the page's address: read their metrics and files, add metrics or files to them. The snippet's header installs what your server needs, your site's package included ([use the runs in a script](/docs/user-guide/sharing#runs-in-scripts)).
- See which metrics a script added to a run after it ran: a small pencil mark next to the value, on run cards and in the Metrics Table and Metrics Diff; hover it to see who added it, when and with which script ([metrics added after the run](/docs/user-guide/metrics#added-metrics)).

### Exports
- See every file's name before you export: **Export** opens a dialog with an example name, each part in its color and labeled (input, settings, file, new or reference), what each part says and the values it takes, and a preview of every name in the same colors. It refuses to export until no two files get the same name, with one-click fixes ([export files](/docs/user-guide/sharing#exporting-files)).
- Get short, readable names: names say only what tells files apart, usually the input, `night_street.png`, then, when needed, the settings that differ between runs on the same input, the file when runs have several, and what the page calls the new and reference results (`new`, a milestone's name, or what you type), e.g. `night_street__v1.2.png`, instead of hashes. Only the runs that need a setting say it: one image that ran with a non-standard crop gets `street__crop=center.png`, the others keep their plain names. Without a reference, names don't talk about new or reference.
- Choose how files are named: all in one folder, a folder for new and one for reference to compare them in a diff tool, folders like the inputs, or your own template with fields like `{side}`, `{input}`, `{settings}`, `{file}` and `{region}`, in **More options** with links or copies and the folder ([choose how files are named](/docs/user-guide/sharing#export-names)).
- Pick the settings names say: the dialog lists the batches of `qaboard.yaml`, databases, parameters, configurations and platforms that differ between the runs shown, and checks those that differ between runs on the same input. Write them `gain=4`, `gain-4` or `4`, or as a short id; for scripts, every name can say every setting, with `default` for runs that don't set it.
- Get names Windows can open, by default: no characters Windows refuses, names that differ only by case count as the same, and paths over 260 characters are flagged with fixes. Turn it off when nobody opens the files on Windows ([names Windows can open](/docs/user-guide/sharing#windows-names)).
- Export crops of the images' regions of interest, with the whole images or alone: the regions shown in the image viewers, the inputs' presets, where images differ from their reference (found in all the runs in one click, the 5 largest differences of each image, or as many as you choose), your site's annotations, or quarters (experimental). Crops are named after their region's label, or `diff-01`, `roi-01`…, and references are cropped at the same place ([export crops](/docs/user-guide/sharing#export-crops)).
- Export the same way again: your last export's settings come back in each project, and you can save presets, for all projects or one, e.g. for what a script expects. Presets that would give files the same name on the page are disabled and say why ([save your export settings](/docs/user-guide/sharing#export-presets)).
- Export only the new results or only the reference, in one click under the pattern, call new and reference something else for one export, and choose what's before them in names, e.g. `night_street@new.png` ([export files](/docs/user-guide/sharing#exporting-files)).
- Keep your scripts working: **Export (legacy)** is the previous export, unchanged, with the same names and folders. It stays until the new export does what you need: after an export, **Tell us** sends your feedback.
- Keep track of your exports: each one goes to a new folder that you can name, listed with its name in **Storage usage → Exports**, and `0.export.csv` in the folder lists each file with its run, input, settings and region.

### Runs activity
- Find where pending runs come from: click a batch, commit, branch, queue, runner, platform, user or `qa batch` command in **Runs activity** to see only its runs, in the totals, the timeline and the lists, or choose **Only these runs** in a row's menu. Each filter is a tag you remove in one click, and you can group runs by **Branch** too ([runs activity](/docs/user-guide/runs-activity#find-where-runs-come-from)).
- List the runs **pending for over a day**, the oldest first: click their card, or choose them in **Status**. **Mark as failed…** then marks only those, and the list of runs sorts them the oldest first too.
- Read the timeline: it shows the most runs pending at once, the time of day, a tooltip on the ended runs, and failures that stand out. Its bars start on the hour.
- Know when there's nothing to show: without runs, the page says so, and offers the last 7 days, everyone's runs, or to clear the filters. While it loads, the cards and the timeline keep their place instead of a spinner, so the page no longer jumps.

### Storage usage
- Look at one person's runs: choose **Someone…** and search for their name, or type the user name their runs were made with, in **Storage usage** and **Runs activity**. Admins see anyone's usage, developers and owners other people's usage in their projects ([storage usage](/docs/user-guide/storage-usage#find-what-takes-space)).
- Find the exports made before QA-Board tracked them in **Storage usage → Exports** after a storage scan, with their project, commit and batch. Those in a folder someone chose are tagged: deleting one deletes the whole folder, so only admins and whoever owns all its files can ([exports](/docs/user-guide/storage-usage#exports)).
- Admins see everyone's untracked files in **Storage usage → Not tracked** when they choose *Everyone's*, with their owners.

### Running tests
- Deal with many failed runs at once: when more than 5 runs failed, the **N failed** message no longer lists them all above the results. **Show only the failed runs** filters the page to them (with `status:failed`, a new filter word), and **List the N runs** opens the list. With a few failed runs, a button next to each one filters the page to it ([the commit page](/docs/user-guide/commit-page)).
- See where a batch is defined at a glance: **Run Tests / Tuning**, **Available Tests** and the **Logs** name each batch file once, e.g. *Defined in qa/batches.yaml*, instead of listing every batch an alias or a pattern stands for. Click a file to open it on your git server at the commit, or the shared and private batches in **Available Tests**, at the batch ([running tests](/docs/user-guide/running-from-the-webapp#running-tests-and-tuning)).

### Your account
- Change the password of your QA-Board account in **Settings → Sign-in methods**, e.g. the one an admin gave you: your other browsers are signed out ([your account](/docs/user-guide/account#settings)).

### Your usage
- See your own usage of QA-Board in **Settings → Activity**: the pages you opened, what you started and the `qa` commands you ran in the last 30 days.

### Runs' settings
- Read long settings in full, e.g. whole commands passed as settings: click a setting shortened with `…` to show all of them in full, in every run, and click again to shorten them. The web app remembers your choice, also in **Settings → Preferences** ([read configurations](/docs/user-guide/comparing-results#reading-configurations)).

### People
- See people's pictures next to their names: in the account menu, **Settings → Profile** (with your job title, department and organization), project **Access** pages and the member picker, and the admin pages. They come from your company's sign-in, single sign-on, LDAP or SAML, and are updated each time you sign in ([your account](/docs/user-guide/account)).

- Let GitHub Actions or GitLab CI jobs send results as a service account without storing any token: open the service account's **Tokens**, click **Trust CI jobs**, choose the repository and, if you want, a branch or environment, and copy the CI configuration it shows ([upload from CI without a secret](/docs/ci-integration#ci-without-secrets)).

## CLI and project setup

### CI without stored secrets
- In GitHub Actions (with `permissions: id-token: write`) or GitLab CI (with `id_tokens: QABOARD_ID_TOKEN`), `qa` exchanges the job's ID token for a short-lived token of the service account that trusts the job, by itself: no `QABOARD_TOKEN` to store. `QABOARD_SERVICE_ACCOUNT` picks the account when several trust the job ([upload from CI without a secret](/docs/ci-integration#ci-without-secrets)).

### Results from Python
- Get the runs a web app page shows from Python with `qaboard.runs(url)`, or name them with `runs(project=, commit=, batch=, filter=)`: `Run` objects named like the `context` of `run()` (`input`, `configs`, `params`, `metrics`, `output_dir`, `state`), filtered like the web app. From the shell, `qa runs <url>` prints one folder per line, or everything with `--json`, and works outside a project ([runs in scripts](/docs/apis#runs-in-scripts)).
- Add metrics and files to runs from a script: `run.log_metrics({"sharpness": 0.4})`, signed in with a token with the `ingest` scope, merges the metrics into the run's and records who added each one, when and with which script (wrap values in `Metric(...)` to declare them; the run's own metrics are only replaced with `overwrite=True`, and win when it runs again). `run.log_artifact("sharpness/map.png", file)` adds a file to the run's folder, never overwriting, and the web app lists it at once. `runs(url, dry_run=True)` prints what a script would do ([add metrics](/docs/apis#log), [add files](/docs/apis#log-artifact)).
- Import `qaboard` in any script: `import qaboard` no longer reads the current folder's *qaboard.yaml* and git, nor checks for updates (the `qa` command still does), so a broken *qaboard.yaml* elsewhere doesn't break `from qaboard import Metric`.
- Filters match the same runs everywhere: the web app, `qaboard.runs()`, exports and moves of filtered runs, and milestones' summaries share one implementation. On the server, filters gained what the web app's had: `status:success`, `status:failed`, `id:<run id>` (now exactly this run, not 12345 for `id:1234`), the platform and the parameters runs report; words starting with `-` exclude plain text, and an invalid regular expression matches loosely instead of failing.

### Results over HTTP
- Read results from any language: your server describes its HTTP API at `/api/v1/openapi.json` (OpenAPI 3.1), for client generators and agents, and `/api/v1/docs` lists the endpoints with their parameters and answers, and tries requests with your session or a token ([HTTP API](/docs/http-api#reference)).

### Declaring metrics in your code
- Declare your metrics where your code computes them: return `Metric(value, suffix=" ms", smaller_is_better=True, target=15)`, call `define_metric("psnr", smaller_is_better=False)`, or write a *metrics_definitions.json* next to *metrics.json* from any language, checked in your editor with its JSON schema. Only the metrics a run returns get definitions: `qa` saves them in the run's *metrics_definitions.json*, so `qa postprocess` and `qa sync` keep them, and QA-Board keeps them with each commit ([declaring metrics](/docs/computing-quantitative-metrics#declaring-metrics)).
- Keep your metrics file while you move: for a metric declared in both, the fields from your code win, and `summary_metrics`, `main_metrics` and the other lists can name metrics the file doesn't declare. The metrics file `qa init` creates now has QA-Board's choices, not only `is_failed`.
- Write `smaller_is_better: null` in a metrics file for metrics where neither is better, like counts: their changes stay neutral. If your metrics file has `smaller_is_better: null`, or `smaller_is_better:` with nothing after it, write `true`: they used to mean "smaller is better" ([metrics files](/docs/computing-quantitative-metrics#declaring-metrics-in-a-metrics-file)).
- Use the metrics your code declares in `qa gate`'s regression rules: the gate reads which way is better like the web app ([quality gate](/docs/quality-gate#rules)).

### Automated tuning with `qa optimize`
- Search with Optuna: `qa optimize` no longer uses scikit-optimize, which isn't maintained anymore, and is no longer experimental. Configurations keep working except the `solver` settings, and `qa optimize` says what to write instead. `solver.sampler` picks Gaussian processes (with PyTorch), TPE or random search ([from scikit-optimize](/docs/auto-optimization#scikit-optimize), [solver](/docs/auto-optimization#solver)).
- Say which way is better in the objective: `ap_small: max`, or `model_mb: {goal: min, weight: 0.005}`. Without it, `qa optimize` uses what your code declares, even when it's only known once the code ran, then your metrics file, and when nothing says, stops with what to write. `target` gives what the `shift` and `relative` losses compare to ([the objective](/docs/auto-optimization#objective)).
- Resume an automated tuning by running it again: it saves its progress and continues where it stopped, also with a bigger `evaluations` budget or wider ranges. `--checkpoint` is optional ([resume](/docs/auto-optimization#resume)).
- Search faster and stop in time: with `parallel_sampling`, a new evaluation starts as soon as one ends; `early_stopping` stops a search that stopped improving; `pareto: true` looks for the best trade-offs between metrics. A failed evaluation is recorded, its runs kept for their logs, and the search goes on. Configuration errors are all reported before the first evaluation, with where they are ([configuration](/docs/auto-optimization#configuration)).

### Runs that are done again
- Shared and CI runs that are done again are saved in a new folder, `<run>_attempt2`, `_attempt3`..., and the previous attempt's files are removed, except its log, kept as *log.attempt1.txt*. The first attempt is where runs always were, with a *latest_attempt.txt* when there are others: existing results need no change, and `qa postprocess`, `qa batch --list-output-dirs`, `qa get output_dir` and `qa check-bit-accuracy` find the latest attempt, also offline. Local runs are still done again in place. Scripts that build results' paths themselves read *latest_attempt.txt*, or the project opts out with `outputs: {reruns: in-place}` ([when a run is done again](/docs/storage/where-is-the-data#reruns)).

### Quality gate
- Get the gate's verdict where changes are reviewed: `qa gate --post-comment github` (or `gitlab`) posts its table on the commit's pull request and updates it on each push. The comment, like the terminal's output, says when runs only passed after being done again, and when the commit loosens the gate's rules compared with the reference's ([comment on pull requests](/docs/quality-gate#pull-request-comments)).

### Usage events
- `qa` tells the server which commands people run (the command, version, project, runner, duration and exit code), outside CI only, in the background: it adds at most a second when the server is slow, and `QA_NO_ANALYTICS=1` keeps it quiet ([details](/docs/backend-admin/usage-analytics)).

### Runners
- Run batches on Kubernetes 1.29 or later with the `kubernetes` runner (`pip install "qaboard[kubernetes]"`): each run is a pod of a Kubernetes Job, running as your own user, with your shared storage mounted, within your cluster's queues and quotas. When pods wait, `qa batch` says why and what to do ([Kubernetes](/docs/kubernetes-runner)).
- Keep GPU pods busy with short runs: `runs_per_pod` lets each pod do several runs, one after the other, each within its `timeout`, the pod within its `pod_timeout` ([several runs per pod](/docs/kubernetes-runner#runs-per-pod)).
- Start Dask workers on Slurm, PBS, SGE, HTCondor, OAR, Moab or Kubernetes, not only LSF, with `runners.dask.cluster.type` ([other schedulers](/docs/dask-integration#other-schedulers)).
- Stop waiting for Dask workers that never start: `runners.dask.worker_start_timeout` gives up after that many seconds (by default 15 minutes on Kubernetes and with `local`), and `qa batch` stops with the scheduler's error when it refuses the worker jobs ([how it works](/docs/dask-integration#how-it-works)).
- Check everything a batch needs with `qa doctor`: *qaboard.yaml*, the server, the storage and the runner's permissions, with how to fix each problem. `qa doctor --test-job` also starts a small job ([qa doctor](/docs/doctor)).
- Stop a Celery batch with Ctrl+C: `qa batch` now drops its waiting tasks and stops the running ones, with everything their `qa run` started, instead of leaving them running ([stop a batch](/docs/celery-integration#stop-a-batch)).
- Keep more secrets out of your runs: the `celery` runner uses the same filter as the `kubernetes` runner. Besides names with `TOKEN`, `ACCESS`, `AUTH` or `SECRET`, it no longer forwards names with `PASSWORD`, `PASSWD`, `CREDENTIAL`, `PRIVATE`, `COOKIE`, `JWT` or `APIKEY`, names with the words `KEY`, `PASS`, `PW` or `SESSION` (like `OPENAI_API_KEY`), nor URLs with credentials. Name those your runs need in `runners.celery.forward_secrets` ([secrets](/docs/celery-integration#secrets)).
- Give Celery runs what only the workers know, like secrets or `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES`: runs now start from the worker's environment, with the variables of `qa batch` on top ([Celery](/docs/celery-integration)).

## Server and administration

### Runners
- Let users start, redo and stop batches on Kubernetes from the web app, as themselves, with `QABOARD_TUNING_RUNNER=kubernetes` (as their image's user only if you allow it, with `QABOARD_KUBERNETES_ALLOW_RUN_AS_IMAGE`). Give each team its own namespace: users who start batches in a namespace can act as each other there ([Run batches from the web app](/docs/backend-admin/runners#kubernetes)).
- Set it up with the Helm chart: `runners.kubernetes.enabled` and `namespaces` create the Role, the ServiceAccounts and the RoleBindings in each namespace, and set the backend's variables ([Kubernetes](/docs/backend-admin/kubernetes#start-batches-from-the-web-app)).
- Stop Dask batches from the web app when their workers run on Kubernetes (the chart's `runners.kubernetes.dask`), or on Slurm, PBS, SGE, HTCondor or OAR when the backend's image has their commands ([stop runs](/docs/backend-admin/runners#stop)).
- Stop Celery batches from the web app: **Stop runs** revokes the batch's tasks on the server's broker, and workers stop the running ones with what they started ([stop runs](/docs/backend-admin/runners#stop)).
- Stop the batches the server runs itself, with `QABOARD_TUNING_RUNNER=local` or `celery`: **Stop runs** stops their `qa batch` and the runs it started on the server. With several replicas of the backend, users are told when it runs on another one ([stop runs](/docs/backend-admin/runners#stop)).
- Keep the server's secrets out of the `qa batch` it runs for users with `local` or `celery`: it no longer gets the server's passwords, keys, tokens, database and sign-in settings. The projects' code still runs in the server's container: use `lsf` or `kubernetes` when you don't trust every developer with it ([security](/docs/backend-admin/runners#security)).

### Settings and secrets
- Change most of the server's settings in **Admin → Settings**, without a deploy: git hosts and their tokens, Jenkins servers, email and the SMTP password, the web app's support link, docs and avatars, PostHog, the image cache... Settings are grouped, searchable (press <kbd>/</kbd>) and filtered (**Changed**, **From the environment**, **Secrets**, **Needs attention**), and each says what it does, its default and why. Click its badge to see where its value comes from: the server's environment, which still wins, what was saved here and by whom, or the default ([settings](/docs/backend-admin/settings)).
- Move settings out of the environment, even the JSON ones like `JENKINS_AUTH`, `GITLAB_AUTH` and `QABOARD_GIT_HOSTS`: **Save it here** copies a variable's value to the database, then you remove it. Lists of servers are edited in a form, and **Send a test email** checks the SMTP settings ([move a setting](/docs/backend-admin/settings#move-a-setting-from-the-environment-to-the-database)).
- Store tokens and passwords safely: secrets are encrypted in the database, never sent back to the browser, and never in the audit log. The key is never in the database: it comes from `QABOARD_ENCRYPTION_KEY` (or `QABOARD_ENCRYPTION_KEY_FILE`), else the server generates one in its data folder, and the Helm chart generates one in its secret. **Admin → Settings → Security** shows the key's fingerprint and the secrets it decrypts, asks you to back up a generated key, finds secrets a lost key made unreadable, and re-encrypts them when you change the key with `QABOARD_ENCRYPTION_KEY_PREVIOUS` ([the encryption key](/docs/backend-admin/settings#secrets-and-the-encryption-key)).
- Manage the settings from the server's shell with `qaboard-admin settings list|set|reset|export|import`, e.g. to copy them to a staging server, and the key with `qaboard-admin encryption status|rotate|show-key` ([from the command line](/docs/backend-admin/settings#from-the-command-line)).
- Sign-in providers' client secrets and refresh tokens no longer need `QABOARD_ENCRYPTION_KEY` to be set first: without it, the server generates a key.

### Accounts and sign-in
- Set up a new server from the web app: it opens **Set up QA-Board**, where the setup token from the backend's logs creates the first admin. No shell needed, and local sign-up waits until it's done. Automated installs set `QABOARD_ADMIN_PASSWORD` (or `QABOARD_ADMIN_PASSWORD_FILE`) instead ([the first admin](/docs/backend-admin/user-management#the-first-admin)).
- Create local accounts in **Admin → Users → New user**, with a password QA-Board generates for you to give, or one you choose, and give forgotten passwords a new one with **Reset password** on the user's page ([local accounts](/docs/backend-admin/user-management#local-accounts)).
- Keep users' profiles up to date from their sign-in: at each sign-in, and daily for LDAP, QA-Board updates their name, verified email, job title, department, organization and picture. Say which claim or attribute holds the picture when it isn't the standard one (`QABOARD_OIDC_PICTURE_CLAIM`, `QABOARD_LDAP_ATTRIBUTE_PICTURE`, `QABOARD_SAML_ATTRIBUTE_PICTURE`), or read photos from an URL with the user's access token, e.g. Microsoft Graph's for Entra ID (`QABOARD_OIDC_PICTURE_URL`, or *Picture URL* in a provider's settings). QA-Board serves the pictures itself ([pictures and profiles](/docs/backend-admin/sign-in#pictures-and-profiles)).
- Reorder the sign-in methods in **Admin → Sign-in** with **Move up** and **Move down**, the environment's too: the sign-in page follows that order. It replaces the *Position* of OIDC providers ([several providers](/docs/backend-admin/sign-in#several-providers)).
- CI jobs' exchanged tokens (`qab_tmp_`) last `tokens.ci_lifetime_minutes`, 6 hours by default. Exchanges are rate-limited like sign-ins and audited as `token.exchange`; the server needs to reach the CI's issuer (GitHub, or your GitLab). Projects' owners can trust GitHub, GitLab.com and your configured git hosts, other issuers need an admin ([service accounts](/docs/backend-admin/user-management#service-accounts)).

### Usage analytics
- See how QA-Board is used in **Admin → Usage**: people active in the web app and in the `qa` CLI, commits looked at, comparisons, outputs and image viewer tools used, batches started from the web app and from terminals, exports and milestones, as headline numbers, trends, and leaderboards of users, groups and projects, each with a **CSV** export. An adoption table tells the projects nobody looks at from the active ones ([usage analytics](/docs/backend-admin/usage-analytics)).
- **Get more from QA-Board**, on the admin overview: what isn't configured yet (email notifications, single sign-on, git host webhooks, Sentry, annotation sources, a storage cleaning schedule, project owners), and what the usage says, next to the security checklist. **Active in the last 30 days** now counts the people who used QA-Board, not only those who signed in.
- Query the usage from Metabase or SQL: the `usage_events` table and the `usage_events_v` and `usage_daily_v` views, or export it with `qaboard-admin usage export`. Turn it all off with `analytics.enabled` (`QABOARD_ANALYTICS_ENABLED=false`).

### Storage and exports
- Exports from the web app go to a new folder each time, `<date>-<name>-<id>`, in `storage.exports_root` or next to the commit's results. The web app uses new APIs, `POST /api/v1/exports/preview` and `POST /api/v1/exports`, where the caller says how to name files. The older `/api/v1/export`, which scripts call, works as before and is deprecated: its answers have a `Deprecation` header, to find its callers ([exports](/docs/backend-admin/storage#exports)).
- Storage scans track every export they find, so their owners see them: the scans' **Track** action is gone ([scanning the storage](/docs/backend-admin/storage#scanning-the-storage)).

### Integrations
- Show the annotations of another app in the image viewer: install a Python package with an annotation source, a module that finds the annotations of an image ([annotation sources](/docs/backend-admin/annotations)).
- Find who a web app error happened to in Sentry: its events carry the username, like the backend's, and say which part of the app crashed (the `error_boundary` tag). Users' notes on what they were doing come as user feedback, linked to the error ([monitoring](/docs/backend-admin/monitoring)).
- The API's description, `/api/v1/openapi.json`, and its reference page, `/api/v1/docs`, are public, also when sign-in is required: they describe the API, not the data. The backend installs `flask-swagger-ui` for the page's files, so it works without internet access.
- Scripts can add metrics to finished runs with `PATCH /api/v1/output/<id>/metrics` (what `run.log_metrics()` calls), with a token with the `ingest` scope and the developer role, whatever `ingest.auth` says. The provenance is kept in the run's data, nothing is written to the storage, and a run's own metrics win when it runs again.
- Tell scripts what to install besides `qaboard`, e.g. your site package: `web.cli_requirements` in **Admin → Settings → Web app** (or `QABOARD_CLI_REQUIREMENTS`), pip requirements. The web app's **Script** snippets and install hint use them ([server settings](/docs/deploy#optional-environment-variables)).
- Let scripts start Jenkins jobs without a token for a while, while they move to tokens: list texts their job paths contain in `jenkins.anonymous_jobs` (or `QABOARD_JENKINS_ANONYMOUS_JOBS`); their callers get a warning, and refused ones are told how to send a token ([Jenkins jobs](/docs/backend-admin/user-management#jenkins-jobs-started-without-a-token), [authentication](/docs/apis#authentication)).
- The backend installs Optuna instead of scikit-optimize, and checks tuning configurations before starting them, with `qa optimize`'s own messages.

### Deployment
- Smaller Docker images, quicker to build, pull and deploy: the backend no longer ships compilers (2.2 GB → 1.4 GB), the frontend and docs images only hold their bundles (the docs image: 1.3 GB → 40 MB), the image server ships a Java runtime instead of a JDK, and the proxy runs nginx 1.22 on Debian 12 instead of 1.14 on Debian 10, which no longer gets updates. Release notes no longer make the frontend's build reinstall its dependencies. If you build the images with `development.yml`, it now builds their `dev` stages, which keep the compilers and `node_modules`.

## Performance
- **Runs activity** loads 3 to 9 times faster on busy servers: its numbers, timeline and busiest lists read the runs once instead of once each. With everyone's runs on a server that runs 80,000 a day, its overview takes 0.7 s instead of 2.2 s for the last 24 hours, and 3.5 s instead of 13 s for 7 days.

## Fixes
- Batches started from the web app with `QABOARD_TUNING_RUNNER=local` or `celery`, and runs redone without an LSF bridge, are no longer killed after 5 minutes, and the web app no longer waits for them: `qa batch` runs in the background on the server ([on the server itself](/docs/backend-admin/runners#local)).
- The web app says where `qa batch` runs for batches started from QA-Board: on the server (with its process id), in a Kubernetes job (and why its pod waits), or in an LSF job, and shows stopped ones as **Stopped**.
- An invalid `JENKINS_AUTH` or `GITLAB_AUTH` no longer stops the server from starting: it's ignored, and the admin overview says what's wrong with it. Jenkins servers that don't require a CSRF crumb no longer need one in their credentials.
- Export copies of files when the server knows your account only from its directory (`QABOARD_ACCOUNTS_COMMAND`): exports failed with `getpwnam(): name not found`. If QA-Board doesn't know your account at all, the export preview says so, and you can export links instead.
- Without a default metric, runs are sorted by name again, A to Z: **Name (default)** sorted them by run id, newest first.
- Rename a reference from its tab's **⋯** menu, or right after **Duplicate**: the name field closed as soon as it opened.
- The sidebar's **Runs activity** submenu opens next to the sidebar, like the others: it opened off-screen, on the left.
- In **Storage usage → Users**, the bars show each person's share of the total, like the **Share** column: they compared people to the biggest user.
- **Storage usage** no longer crashes when you go from the Batches tab to Commits. It and **Runs activity** no longer show your own numbers under **Everyone's** while everyone's load, and **Runs activity** says why it could not load instead of going back to a spinner every 15 seconds.
- `qa batch` with LSF no longer gets stuck after sending its runs when started from a Docker container: it waits for them with `bwait` instead of an interactive LSF job, and no longer adds a WAIT job to the queue.
- The backend image upgrades `qaboard` behind a proxy whose CA you install with `CA_CERT_URL`: uv now trusts the system's certificates, like the rest of the backend.
- `qa batch` no longer crashes when killed runs left an empty or corrupt `metrics.json`: those runs are marked failed and the batch continues.
- Type `/` and `?` in the **Available Tests** editor and the other code editors: in Chrome and Edge, they opened the command palette and the cheat sheet instead.
- Edit large batch files in **Available Tests** without the page freezing for seconds after some deletes, undos or new lines: files that reuse YAML anchors in many batches (`<<: *defaults`) were checked in a time that grew with the square of their size.
