Was this commit better? This page answers that question for one real commit of the demo data, in ten short steps.
On the way, you meet the parts of the web app you'll use every day.

The camera team of Kitefin Robotics, a fictional company, works on `kitefin/camera-isp`: the image pipeline of their
robot's camera, from the raw sensor data to the displayed image. Ines replaced its denoiser with a bilateral filter.
Her commit said "+1.6 dB PSNR in low light", and she merged it into `main`. Check it yourself.

:::info Before you start
You need a QA-Board server with the demo projects: [run one on your computer](/docs/get-started/quickstart), then
open [http://localhost:5151](http://localhost:5151). Steps [7](#step-7-see-which-runs-changed-most) and
[9](#step-9-save-a-milestone) need you to sign in: click **Sign in** (top right of Explore, or at the bottom of the
sidebar) and sign in as `ines`, password `kitefin-demo`. Every person of the demo world has an account.
:::

## 1. Open the project {#step-1-open-the-project}
On Explore (**Explore** in the top bar, or the home page when you are signed out), click **camera-isp** in the **kitefin** group. You get its commits with results, on every branch, newest first,
grouped by day. Signed in, the page starts with your own batches: click **Everyone** above the list to see every
commit.

<Shot
  id="projects/explore"
  maxWidth={700}
  caption="Click a project's name to see its commits. The star pins a project on top of the list."
/>

The list shows the last 3 days at first: the demo history ends when your QA-Board first started, so you see its
latest commits. If it started days ago, the list may be empty: the search in step 2 looks at every date. More in
[Finding results](/docs/user-guide/finding-results#projects-list).

## 2. Find the commit {#step-2-find-the-commit}
1. Type `bilateral` in the **Search commits** box at the top right. After a moment, the list shows every commit that
   mentions it, on any branch and at any date.
2. Find **Merge branch 'isp/bilateral-denoise'**, on `main`, and click **26 runs** on the right of its row.

The commit page opens. More in [Searching commits](/docs/user-guide/finding-results#searching-commits).

## 3. Compare it with the release {#step-3-compare-with-the-release}
The page has two bars at the top: the commit you opened, tagged **new**, and below it the reference it's compared
with, tagged **ref**. By default, the reference is the latest commit with results on the project's reference branch,
`main`. For a commit in the past, that's a later one. To judge the denoiser, compare it with the release before it,
which the team saved as a milestone:

1. In the reference bar, click **Change**.
2. Under **Shared with the project**, click **isp-4.1**.

The reference bar now shows the release, *isp: release 4.1, gaussian denoise and detail boost 1.2*, with a crown:
it's a milestone shared with the project. Tables and viewers now call it **isp-4.1**.

More in [Picking a reference](/docs/user-guide/comparing-results#picking-a-reference).

## 4. Read the metrics {#step-4-read-the-metrics}
The page opened on the **Summary**: each main metric's median over the runs, new against reference, with its change,
green when it gets better and red when it gets worse. Medians say that something moved. To see which runs moved:

1. Click **Metrics Diff** in the sidebar: one row per run, the change of each metric.
2. Type `base linux` in the **Filter runs…** box of the new commit's bar. The table keeps the 10 runs at ISO 100, on
   the float pipeline (the `hw-model` platform runs the camera chip's fixed-point model).

On the facade photo, PSNR is up 1.34 dB (5%). On the slanted-edge chart, PSNR is up too, but **Overshoot** is up
163%, in red: the sharpened edge now overshoots by 11% of its step, against about 4% in the release. That's what halos
look like in numbers.

More in [Reading metrics](/docs/user-guide/metrics#metrics-diff).

## 5. Look at the images {#step-5-look-at-the-images}
1. Click **Visualizations** in the sidebar, and type `facade` before `base linux` in the filter box.
2. On the card of **photos/facade.png**, click the preset **Window frame (halos)**, then scroll on an image to zoom
   and drag it to move.

Each card is one run: its main metrics against the reference, then its images, new and reference side by side. When
you zoom into one image, the other follows.

<Shot
  id="commit/visualizations"
  caption="The facade's card: PSNR and SSIM up against the release, the regions the team saved for this photo, then the new and reference images, zoomed in together."
/>

More in [Synced zoom and pan](/docs/user-guide/image-viewer#synced-zoom-and-pan).

## 6. Show their difference {#step-6-show-their-difference}
1. In the card's toolbar, click **Difference**. Their difference shows between the images: small differences, like
   the noise the denoisers leave, color it everywhere.
2. In the menu next to **Perceptual**, pick **20%**.

Small differences fade. What's left outlines the window frame: the halos that PSNR missed.

<Shot
  id="commit/image-perceptual-diff"
  caption="At 20%, the flat wall fades to white: only the edges of the window frame, where the halos are, stay colored."
/>

More in [The difference](/docs/user-guide/image-viewer#the-difference).

## 7. See which runs changed most (optional) {#step-7-see-which-runs-changed-most}
Did other images change as much?

1. Remove `facade` from the filter box, so the page shows the 10 runs again.
2. On any card, open the regions menu at the right of the toolbar (it says **Presets**), and click **Where they
   differ, in all 10 runs…**

The server compares each run's images with the reference's, and ranks the runs. **Show** scrolls to a run's card
and zooms on the regions found.

<Shot
  id="commit/regions-all-runs"
  caption="The Siemens star, the backlit window and the facade changed most; the flat gray chart barely moved."
/>

More in [Which runs changed most](/docs/user-guide/image-viewer#which-runs-changed-most).

## 8. Find when it started {#step-8-find-when-it-started}
1. On the card of **photos/facade.png**, hover its name, then click **History** (*this input over time*). History
   opens: the facade's metrics over the commits of `main`, from 3 days before the merge to today.
2. Hover the points of the PSNR chart, from right to left.

<Shot
  id="commit/run-name-menu"
  maxWidth={380}
  caption="The menu of a run's name: History is at the top."
/>

Below the chart, the hovered commit shows as **New** and the commit before it as **Reference**, with their images.
Where the PSNR jumps, the new commit is the merge, and the leaves get halos.

<Shot
  id="history/image-diff"
  caption="The facade's PSNR jumps at the merge of the bilateral denoiser; hovering that point compares its images with the commit before: only the new one outlines the leaves."
/>

Press your browser's **Back** button to return to the commit page. More in
[History](/docs/user-guide/history-and-dashboards).

## 9. Save it as a milestone {#step-9-save-a-milestone}
A milestone keeps these results, so that you can compare the fix with them later.

1. Clear the filter box (its **✕**): a milestone keeps the batch and the filter you're looking at.
2. Click the star next to the new commit's hash.
3. Name it, like `bilateral, with halos`, leave **Only me** selected, and click **Save milestone**.

The star turns gold, and the name shows next to it. The milestone is saved in your account: you'll find it under
**My milestones**, in the **Change** menus and in the sidebar's **Milestones**. More in
[Milestones](/docs/user-guide/milestones#saving-a-milestone).

## 10. Share what you found {#step-10-share}
Copy the page's link from your browser, and paste it in your bug report or chat. The link keeps the commits, the
reference, the filter, the view and how images are compared, but not the zoom: whoever opens it gets the same
comparison, even after `main` moves on. To point at a spot, paste a copy of the image too (the copy button above the
images), or name the region, e.g. *Window frame (halos)*.

To share the milestone, click its star again, then **Copy link**. More in [Sharing](/docs/user-guide/sharing).

## Check the fix {#check-the-fix}
Ten days later, Ines merged a fix: near strong edges, the sharpening now stays within the range of the neighboring
pixels. Compare it with your milestone:

1. Click the project's name, **camera-isp**, in the sidebar: its commits show again. Search for `halo-clamp`, and
   open the runs of **Merge branch 'isp/halo-clamp'**.
2. In the reference bar, click **Change**, then your milestone under **My milestones**.

The edge overshoot is back to about 5%, and on the facade, the dark rims around the leaves fade.

## What else you can do {#what-else}
- **Compare with several references at once**, like isp-4.1 and the latest `main`: hover the reference bar, then
  **Stack a reference**. See [Several references](/docs/user-guide/comparing-results#several-references).
- **Run more tests, or tune parameters**, from the web app, on any commit: see
  [Running from the web app](/docs/user-guide/running-from-the-webapp#running-tests-and-tuning).
- **Find why a run failed**: on the camera project, search `one tile`, open *isp: process frames in one tile*, then
  its **Logs**: the 113 MP test wall's run was killed at its 3 GB memory limit. See
  [Logs and failed runs](/docs/user-guide/logs#why-did-my-run-fail).
- **Compare videos frame by frame**: on the merge commit, against isp-4.1, pick the **video** batch in both bars. See
  [The video viewer](/docs/user-guide/video-viewer).
- **Put your own project on QA-Board**, and fail CI when a metric gets worse: see the
  [tutorial](/docs/get-started/tutorial) and the [quality gate](/docs/quality-gate) (experimental).
