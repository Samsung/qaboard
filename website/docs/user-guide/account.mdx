Sign in, to redo runs, tune and save milestones, keep your settings on every computer, and create tokens for your
scripts and CI.

## Signing in
Sign in with the **Sign in** button, at the top right of Explore, or at the bottom of a project's sidebar. Depending
on your server, you use your company's single sign-on (its button comes first), your directory account (e.g. your
Windows credentials), or a QA-Board account. If your server requires signing in, you see the sign-in page first, and come back
to the page you asked for.

When something goes wrong, the message says what to do: a wrong password, a disabled account (ask your QA-Board
admins), or a sign-in to start again. Single sign-on errors come with a **reference**: give it to your admins, it
tells them what happened.

**If your account waits for an admin**, you see a page saying so after you sign in. Say why you need QA-Board (your
team, your projects): the admins are told, and you get an email when they decide.

Signed in, what you save follows your account: your [milestones](/docs/user-guide/milestones), your pinned projects,
the theme and your other preferences.

## Settings
Open **Settings** from your account menu, at the bottom of the sidebar (or the top right of your home page), next to
**Runs activity** and **Storage usage**:
- **Profile**: your name, email and user name, as your server knows them. Add **Your other git emails**, so that the
  commits you made with them count as yours.
- **Preferences**: the theme (**Same as your system**, **Light** or **Dark**), **Compact** or **Comfortable** commit
  lists, the values written on the pixels, the runs' settings... When you're signed in, your preferences and pinned
  projects follow you to other browsers and computers.
- **Access tokens**: tokens for scripts and CI (below).
- **Sessions**: the browsers where you're signed in. Sign out of the ones you don't use anymore, or of all the
  others at once.
- **Sign-in methods**: the ways you sign in to this account. Add your company's single sign-on to the account you
  already have, so you don't get a second one, or remove a method you don't use (you keep at least one).
- **CLI sign-ins**: the computers where you ran `qa auth login`. Revoke the ones you don't use anymore.
- **Activity**: your recent sign-ins, the tokens you created or revoked, and the CLI logins you approved. If you see
  something you didn't do, revoke your tokens and tell your admins.

## Access tokens
Scripts and CI jobs use tokens to act as you. Create one in **Settings → Access tokens**:
- **A name** that says where you'll use it, e.g. "nightly CI of group/repo".
- **What it can do**: **Read** sees projects and results, **Ingest** sends results (`qa run`, `qa batch`), **Act**
  redoes runs, starts or deletes batches and edits milestones. Give it only what it needs.
- **Projects** (optional): restrict it to some projects, e.g. `group/repo` (and its subprojects) or `group/*`.
- **Expires**: in 30, 90 or 365 days, or never if your server allows it.

The token is shown **once**: copy it then, and store it like a password, e.g. in your CI's secrets as `QABOARD_TOKEN`.
The list shows when and from where each token was last used. Revoke a token as soon as you don't need it, or if it
may have leaked: it stops working at once.

See [CI integration](/docs/ci-integration#tokens) and [APIs](/docs/apis#authentication) for how to use tokens.

## Signing in from the qa CLI
Run `qa auth login`, open the link it prints in any browser (it also works when `qa` runs on a server you reach over
SSH), check that the code is the one in your terminal, and approve. See [Signing in](/docs/installation#signing-in).

## Verified runs
Runs sent with a token show who really sent them: hover a run's name on its card, and its **Run Info** reads "Sent by
ines with token nightly CI". The batch logs show a check mark next to the user of each `qa batch`. Runs sent without
a token only carry the user name `qa` found on the computer that ran them: your server can mark them as unverified,
or require tokens to send results.
