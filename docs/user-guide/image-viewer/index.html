The image viewer

Every image a run saves shows next to the same image of its reference run. Flip between them, zoom to the pixel in both at once, read the values of the pixels, and let QA-Board find where they differ.

The basics​

Images show in the Visualizations view of a commit, in each run's card, and in Output Files when you pick an image file.

Pick how to compare​

Above each pair of images, pick a mode, or press its key:

ModeKeyWhat you see
Side by side1New on the left, reference on the right. A cross marks the pixel under your mouse on both.
Wipe2One image over the other: drag the divider across them.
Single3One image at a time, in place. Press t, or click the image, to flip to the other, then to their difference, then back. Small changes jump out, like in a flicker test.
Difference4Where the images differ, between them. Press 4 again to see it alone, larger.

The mode applies to every image viewer of the page, and the page's link keeps it: whoever opens the link sees the same comparison. It's also in the View options, under Viewers › Compare images. The keys work anywhere on the page, not only over an image.

Tell new from reference​

A label on each image names it: new in blue, the reference in its own color, like their bars at the top of the page. When you named what you compare, e.g. with a milestone's name, the labels use those names. They also give each image's size, e.g. 1224×816.

The swap button next to the modes, or t, swaps the sides in every viewer of the page at once, so that they all show the same one first. When both runs saved the same file, you see one image, tagged same as the reference.

Zoom and pan, in sync​

Scroll (or pinch, or press + -) to zoom, drag to pan: the other image follows. So do the other images of the same input on the page, in the other runs' cards and the run's other visualizations, as long as their proportions match, whatever their resolution.

The toolbar shows the zoom: 100% is one image pixel per screen pixel, 1:1 goes there, and a click on the percentage (or 0) fits the whole image again. Zoomed in, a minimap in the corner shows where you are: click or drag it to go elsewhere.

When the images don't pair up
  • When the reference run has no such image, the viewer shows the new image alone, without the modes.
  • Images whose proportions differ are compared at the same width: a warning, different aspect ratios, says their bottoms don't match.
  • In Single mode, pressing 3 again shows the other image, without going to the difference.

A typical review​

In the demo camera pipeline (image quality), a new bilateral denoiser wins 1.6 dB of PSNR in low light and halves the noise. The numbers say merge it. Here is how to check its images against release 4.1 before you agree:

  1. Rank the runs by how much their images changed. In the regions menu above any image, pick Where they differ, in all 10 runs…. The Siemens star, the backlit window and the facade come first; the flat gray chart barely changed. See which runs changed most.
  2. Go where they differ most. Click Show next to photos/facade.png: its card zooms on the region that differs most, #1 · 27.3%, leaves over the wall. ] goes to the next region. See find where images differ.
  3. Flip between them. Press 3 for Single, then t: the image flips from new to reference in place, and your eye catches what moved, a dark rim around every leaf in the new one. See comparison modes.
  4. Measure it. Press 4 for the Difference, and raise its maximum from 5% to 20% (the menu next to Perceptual): the noise fades out, and only the halos along the leaves and the window frame stay. See the difference.
  5. Read the pixels. Hover a rim: the line above the images gives its coordinates, its values in both images and their difference, Δ. Zoom in further and the values are written on the pixels. See pixel values.
  6. Check for clipping. Open the histogram, click Y to show the luminance alone, and drag over the darkest values: stripes mark those pixels on both images, and the share of each image in the range shows below. A larger share in the new image: its rims clip to black. See histograms.
  7. Share what you found. In the copy button's menu, Copy the new image, and paste the crop in the chat or the bug report, with the page's link. See copy and save.

For a call on looks alone, ask a colleague for a blind test.

The team kept the denoiser, and clamped the sharpening near strong edges: the PSNR gain stays, the edge overshoot goes from 11% back to 5%, and the halos fade.

Find where images differ​

The regions button above the images (it shows the name of the set in view, e.g. Presets) zooms on regions of interest, with their thumbnails above the images. Step through them with [ ], the arrows, or a click on a thumbnail. Full image goes back to the whole image.

  • Presets: the regions set for the input, e.g. on the facade Window frame (halos), Leaves over a window and Flat wall (noise). The viewer opens on the first one. Whoever integrates the project defines them, see regions of interest.
  • Where they differ: the server compares the full images, not what your screen shows, and finds the places where they differ most. Each region says how much, e.g. #1 · 27.3%, and darker swatches mark larger differences.

Pick how the server compares them:

RegionsMeasureUse it for
Where they differ: perceptualPerceived color difference (YIQ), pixel by pixel, as a percentageAny visible change: noise, halos, an edge that moved. Start here.
Where they differ: structure (SSIM)Structural similarity (SSIM), on the images about 1000 pixels wide, as 1 − SSIMTexture and sharpness: a denoiser that smears fine detail, a sharpener that rings, while the colors stay.
Where they differ: ΔE 2000CIEDE2000 color difference, about 1000 pixels wide, as a ΔEColor: white balance, the color correction matrix, chroma noise on a color chart.
How the regions are found, and where they apply
  • The regions are spread over the image: an area that differs everywhere, like leaves, gives one region, not twenty. Each shows some context around the difference.
  • The first time the server reads an image takes a few seconds, then it's quick: the server keeps the results.
  • The set you pick applies to every viewer of the same file on the page, in all the runs' cards. Where they differ is for the page where you pick it: on another page, the viewers don't compare their images until you ask, since that compares all its runs again.
  • The server compares images for logged-in users only: logged out, the viewer asks you to log in.
  • Copy the view as a region, at the bottom of the menu, copies the coordinates of what you see, in the format of presets: send them to whoever maintains the project's presets.

Which runs changed most​

In a batch of dozens of inputs, start where the images changed most. Where they differ, in all N runs…, in the regions menu, compares the image of every run of the page with its reference, and ranks the runs by their largest difference. Runs whose images didn't change at all are counted as identical.

Pick how to compare at the top of the list (perceptual, structure (SSIM) or ΔE 2000). Show takes you to a run's card, zoomed on its region that differs most; every viewer of that file shows its own regions too.

While the runs are compared
  • The server compares three runs at a time. The list says which ones it's comparing and for how long, and which wait for their turn. The first comparisons of large images take a few seconds each, and those running together end at about the same time.
  • Closing the list doesn't stop the comparisons: Stop does, and Compare the others picks up where it stopped.
  • The menu item shows when you're logged in and the page has more than one run with a reference to compare.

The difference​

Press 4, or click Difference: the difference shows between the two images, all three in sync, with a cross on the pixel under your mouse in each. Press 4 again, or the three-columns button next to it, to see the difference alone, larger. Where the images don't differ, a faded copy of the image tells you where you are, and a legend says what the colors mean.

Pick how to measure it next to the modes, and its maximum or amplification:

MeasureWhat it showsUse it for
PerceptualHow different the colors look (YIQ), from yellow (a little) to black at the maximum you pick: 1% to 30%, 5% by default.A first look at any change. Raise the maximum to hide the noise and keep what stands out, like halos.
ΔE 2000The CIEDE2000 color difference, up to a maximum of ΔE 1 to 20 (5 by default). Under 1 you can't see it; about 2.3 is barely noticeable.Color: white balance, the color correction matrix, chroma denoising. It tells whether a change is visible.
AbsoluteEach channel of |new − reference|, amplified ×1 to ×64 (×4 by default). Black means identical.Which channel changed: a cast in one channel, a demosaicing change on red and blue.
SignedThe luma (brightness) of new − reference: red where new is brighter, blue where it's darker, amplified.Brightness shifts: the tone curve, the black level, exposure, the bright and dark rims of halos.
Any changeIn red, every pixel whose displayed value changed at all.Changes that should be none, like a refactor. Zoom to 100% or more.
The difference is of what you see

It's computed on what the viewer displays: 8-bit colors, at your screen's resolution. 16-bit images are converted to 8 bits first, and zoomed out, a screen pixel averages many image pixels: small differences can average out. Zoom to 100% or more to compare every pixel, let the server compare the full images (where they differ), and for bit-exact outputs, use the bit accuracy checks.

Reading the difference
  • Hover the difference: the line above the images gives the pixel's values in both images, their difference, and the measure you picked, e.g. perceptual 4.24%.
  • Zoomed in, the values written on the difference's pixels are new − reference.
  • Swapping the sides keeps the difference between the images. It's of the image shown first minus the other: in Signed, red is where the image shown first is brighter, and the legend names it.
  • Copy the difference and Save the difference (PNG) are in the copy button's menu, in Difference mode.

Compare with another file​

To compare an image with its ground truth, the input or another stage, instead of the reference, click vs reference ▾ next to the modes (or vs the name you gave the reference). The menu lists the run's other images, the likeliest first: the same name in another folder (gt/0001.png for out/0001.png), then names that start alike in the same folder. Type to find one, then click it or press Enter. It takes the reference's place in every mode, with its file name on a teal label.

Every run's viewer of that visualization then compares with its own file of that name, so you can go through all the runs of the page. A run without the file compares with its reference, with a warning: no 0001.png here.

  • To check a single run, pick This run under Apply to, at the bottom of the menu: the other runs keep what they compare with. All runs puts them in step again.
  • The undo button next to the menu, or c, goes back to the reference. c again goes back to the file.
  • Runs without a reference can compare with their files too: the menu then reads Compare with…. Runs without other images don't show the menu.

Pixel values​

Hover a pixel: the line above the images gives its coordinates, its values in the new and reference images, and their difference, Δ, with the measure of the difference you picked. A crosshair marks the same pixel on both images.

There are two kinds of values, and the line says which is which:

  • image: the values in the file, as the run saved them: 16-bit, float, raw... The server reads them, and says what they are, e.g. I;16 (16-bit gray) or F (32-bit float).
  • display: the 8-bit color on your screen. To show an image, the server converts it (16-bit to 8-bit, raw to RGB...), and zoomed out, a screen pixel averages many image pixels. It's what you see, not your data. The line lists it only when it differs from the image values.

8-bit images (L, RGB, RGBA) show one set of values, the image's: the screen shows them as they are, and a swatch shows the color. Δ is the difference of the image values when both images have the same size, else of the displayed ones. The measure (perceptual, ΔE...) is always on the displayed colors. Click the line to switch the values to hexadecimal.

Zoom in until the pixels are large enough to hold their numbers (around 3000%) and the values are written on the pixels, in black or white so that you can read them. A badge under the images says which ones you're reading: Values: image (I;16) or Values: displayed, 8-bit. Pick them with the 123 button above the images (Image values, Displayed values or Off); p hides or shows them. Your browser remembers the choice.

Where the values come from
  • The image values arrive by tiles of 64×64 pixels: the first ones can take a moment while the server opens the image, the next ones are quick.
  • When the server can't read the file, the line says unavailable: hover it to see why, and use the displayed values.
  • The server reads the image values for logged-in users only: logged out, you see the displayed values, and the viewer asks you to log in.
  • When the images have different sizes, the line also gives the pixel's coordinates in the reference.
  • The line keeps the last pixel you hovered when you leave the image, so that you can click it.
  • Raw formats need a reader on the server: see the image viewer's setup.

Histograms​

The histogram button shows the red, green, blue and luminance (Y) histograms of what the images show, new in solid lines and the reference dashed. Zoom on a region to see its histograms. Hover them to read the share of the pixels at a value, in each channel. They count the displayed values, from 0 to 255.

Highlight a range of values by dragging on the histogram: stripes mark the pixels in the range on the images, the rest is dimmed, and below the histogram you read the share of each image in the range. A pixel is highlighted when all the channels shown are in the range: click R, G, B or Y above the histogram to show that channel alone, e.g. to find the reds clipped at 255, and click it again to show them all (Shift+click shows or hides one channel). Drag the range's edges to resize it, or its middle to move it. Click the histogram, or press Esc, to clear it.

Adjust the display​

The adjustments button changes how the images look: Brightness, Contrast, Gamma, Sharpen, one Channel (R, G, B or Luma) as gray levels, False colors (Turbo, Viridis or Inferno) and Invert. Raise the gamma to look into the shadows of a night shot at ISO 3200, show one channel to see its noise alone, or spread small gray-level changes over a false-color palette.

The adjustments apply to both images, and to the same visualization in the other runs' cards, so that they stay comparable. Only the display changes: the pixel values, the difference and the histograms are the images'. A dot on the button reminds you they're on; Reset, or a double-click on a slider, puts them back. They're not saved in the link.

Blind test​

To judge images on their looks alone, e.g. to pick between two denoisers with a colleague, hide which is which: click Blind test next to the swap button, or press h. Every viewer of the page shows its two images in a random order, its own, called A and B: in their labels, the values under the pointer and the histograms. Nothing else tells them apart: the labels turn gray, and the links to the files are hidden. Compare them, flip them (in Single, t goes from A to B, then to their difference), and pick the one you prefer.

The same button, now Reveal (or h), shows which was which, e.g. reference · A, without moving the images. Then Done (or h) ends the test: new first again, everywhere.

Full screen​

The full screen button at the right of the viewer's toolbar, or f, gives the whole screen to the images, with the toolbar: the modes, the difference, regions and pixel values all work there. The difference goes under the two images when that makes them larger. Esc or f leaves.

The button at the top right of each card shows the whole card in full screen instead, with its file picker.

Large images​

Images are streamed tile by tile by an image server: the viewers load only what they show, at the resolution they show it. A 113 MP test wall zooms from the whole wall down to single pixels, in both viewers at once.

Good to know
  • When a run saved both out.png and out.tiff, the viewer shows the TIFF.
  • When tiles can't load, a warning says how many, e.g. 2 tiles failed: hover it to see why.
  • The image values under the pointer come by tiles too, see where the values come from.
  • Which image server shows which formats is set up by your admins: see the image viewer's setup.

Copy and save​

The copy button above the images copies the new or reference image, or in Difference mode their difference, as you see them, with the adjustments: paste them in a chat, a slide or a bug report. It also saves them as PNG files, and opens the original files.

  • Copying images needs the web app on https: else, save them.
  • To share the comparison itself, copy the page's link: it keeps the commits, the mode and the difference's settings, but not the zoom. See sharing.
  • To make the region you're looking at a preset, use Copy the view as a region in the regions menu.

Keyboard shortcuts​

The keys work anywhere on the page, also while you scroll. They go to the viewer in full screen, else the one under your mouse, else the one you used last if it's in view, else the first one in view. They're ignored while you type in a text box. The keyboard button in the viewer's toolbar lists them.
